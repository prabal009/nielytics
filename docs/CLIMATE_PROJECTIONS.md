# Climate Scenario Projections (CMIP6 & Trend Extrapolation)

## 1. Architectural Distinction
A foundational principle of NIElytics is the separation of short-term weather forecasts from long-term climate projections:
- **Weather Forecasts (1–14 days)**: Predict specific daily temperatures based on recent meteorological dynamics.
- **Climate Projections (2030–2050+)**: Estimate multi-decadal climatological shifts and temperature anomalies under emission pathways.

---

## 2. CMIP6 / IPCC AR6 Scenarios (South Asia Regional Climatology)
Projections use regional anomalies derived from the IPCC Sixth Assessment Report (AR6 Working Group I, Chapter 11/12):

### SSP1-2.6 (Sustainable Pathway - Aggressive Mitigation)
- Global warming kept below 2.0°C; net-zero emissions reached around 2050.
- South Asia Warming Anomalies relative to baseline:
  - 2030: +1.3°C [1.1°C – 1.6°C]
  - 2040: +1.5°C [1.3°C – 1.8°C]
  - 2050: +1.6°C [1.4°C – 2.0°C]

### SSP2-4.5 (Middle of the Road)
- Current emission trajectory with modest decarbonization after 2050.
- South Asia Warming Anomalies:
  - 2030: +1.4°C [1.2°C – 1.7°C]
  - 2040: +1.9°C [1.6°C – 2.2°C]
  - 2050: +2.4°C [2.0°C – 2.8°C]

### SSP5-8.5 (Fossil-Fueled Development)
- Unconstrained fossil fuel exploitation; rapid economic growth without climate policy.
- South Asia Warming Anomalies:
  - 2030: +1.6°C [1.3°C – 1.9°C]
  - 2040: +2.3°C [1.9°C – 2.7°C]
  - 2050: +3.3°C [2.7°C – 3.9°C]

## 3. Empirical Trend Extrapolation Limits
Theil-Sen robust slopes project linear historical trends to target years. These empirical extrapolations assume stationary warming rates and carry explicit warnings that linear projections cannot model non-linear tipping points or policy changes.
